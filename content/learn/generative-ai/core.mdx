<PlainEnglish>
Most software you've used sorts or predicts things — spam or not spam, which film you'll like. **Generative** models make new things: text, images, audio. They learn the patterns in millions of examples, then produce fresh examples that follow those patterns.

**Think of it like:** A student who has read thousands of recipes can write a new one that has never existed but still makes sense.

**After this lesson you'll be able to** explain how the main kinds of generative model work and which one powers which product.
</PlainEnglish>

## Discriminative vs generative

<Level l="beginner" />

Most of the models in the [ML Essentials](/learn/ml-essentials/how-models-learn/) station were **discriminative**: they learn the *boundary* between classes. "Is this email spam?"

A **generative** model learns the *distribution of the data itself*, so it can produce new examples that look like the training data. "Write a new email that looks like spam." Or a photo, a song, a paragraph, a piece of code.

<Analogy>
A discriminative art critic can tell a Monet from a Van Gogh. A generative artist has learned the style so deeply they can paint a brand-new "Monet". Generating is harder: you have to understand the whole picture, not just the differences.
</Analogy>

## The generative model zoo

<Level l="beginner" />

| Family | Core idea | Strengths | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Autoregressive** | Generate one piece at a time, each conditioned on everything before it | Exact likelihood, scales superbly | LLMs (GPT, Claude, Llama), code, some audio models |
| **VAE** (variational autoencoder) | Compress to a smooth "latent space", then decode samples from it | Stable training, meaningful latent space | Compression stage inside image generators, anomaly detection |
| **GAN** (generative adversarial network) | A generator tries to fool a discriminator in a two-player game | Very sharp images, fast sampling | Faces (StyleGAN), super-resolution, speech vocoders |
| **Diffusion** | Learn to reverse a gradual noising process | Top image and video quality, stable training | Stable Diffusion, DALL·E 3, Imagen, video models |
| **Flow / flow matching** | Learn an invertible path or "velocity field" from noise to data | Fast, high-quality sampling | Newer image and speech generators |

Everything on the LLM line is autoregressive. The rest matter because modern products are **multimodal**: the same app might write text, generate an image and speak an answer.

## Autoencoders and VAEs

<Level l="intermediate" />

An **autoencoder** squeezes its input through a small bottleneck — the **latent code** — and then tries to reconstruct the original. Trained to minimise reconstruction error, it learns a compact representation of the data. The catch: its latent space has gaps, so decoding a random point usually gives garbage.

A **variational autoencoder** fixes that. Its encoder outputs a *distribution* (a mean and a variance) rather than a single point, and training adds a penalty that pulls those distributions toward a standard normal. The latent space becomes smooth and continuous:

- Sample a random point, decode it, and you get a plausible new example.
- Interpolate between the latent codes of two faces and the decoded images morph smoothly from one to the other.

```
Loss = reconstruction error + β · KL( q(z|x) ‖ N(0, I) )
```

The **reparameterisation trick** (z = μ + σ · ε, where ε is random noise) moves the randomness outside the network so gradients can flow through the sampling step.

## GANs

<Level l="intermediate" />

Two networks compete:

- The **generator** turns random noise into fake samples.
- The **discriminator** tries to tell real samples from fakes.

```mermaid caption="A GAN training step. The discriminator learns to catch fakes; the generator learns from the discriminator's verdicts how to make better fakes."
flowchart TD
  N[Random noise z] --> G[Generator]
  G --> F[Fake image]
  R[Real image] --> D{Discriminator<br/>real or fake?}
  F --> D
  D -- "loss: did it spot the fake?" --> D
  D -- "gradient: how to fool it" --> G
```

<Analogy>
A counterfeiter and a detective. Each time the detective spots a flaw, the counterfeiter improves. Eventually the fake notes are indistinguishable from real ones.
</Analogy>

GANs produce very sharp images, but they're notoriously hard to train: **mode collapse** (the generator learns to produce only a few kinds of output), oscillation, and sensitivity to settings.

## Diffusion models

<Level l="intermediate" />

Diffusion is the technique behind today's best image and video generators.

- **Forward process:** take a real image and add a little Gaussian noise, step by step (say 1,000 steps), until it's pure static.
- **Reverse process:** train a network to predict the noise that was added at each step. To generate, start from pure noise and remove it a step at a time.

```mermaid caption="Training learns the right-to-left direction. Generation starts from random noise and denoises step by step, steered by the text prompt."
flowchart LR
  A[Real image] -->|add noise| B[Slightly noisy] -->|add noise| C[Very noisy] -->|add noise| D[Pure noise]
  D -.->|model removes noise<br/>guided by prompt| C2[Less noisy] -.-> B2[Almost clean] -.-> A2[New image]
```

<Analogy>
A sculptor sees a statue inside a block of marble and chips away everything that isn't the statue. A diffusion model starts with a block of noise and chips away the noise, guided by your prompt, until an image remains.
</Analogy>

**Stable Diffusion** makes this practical by running diffusion in the compressed latent space of a VAE (a **latent diffusion model**, about 50× cheaper than working on raw pixels). It steers generation with text through a text encoder and **classifier-free guidance**, whose *guidance scale* trades how closely the image follows the prompt against how varied the results are.

```python title="text_to_image.py"
import torch
from diffusers import AutoPipelineForText2Image

pipe = AutoPipelineForText2Image.from_pretrained(
    "stabilityai/sdxl-turbo", torch_dtype=torch.float16).to("cuda")
img = pipe("isometric illustration of a data centre in Bengaluru, soft lighting",
           num_inference_steps=2, guidance_scale=0.0).images[0]   # Turbo models need very few steps
img.save("datacentre.png")
```

## How generated output is evaluated

<Level l="intermediate" />

| Modality | Common metrics |
|---|---|
| Images | **FID** (distance between real and generated feature distributions; lower is better), **CLIP score** (how well the image matches the text), human preference |
| Text | Perplexity, task benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval), LLM-as-judge, human evaluation |
| Speech | MOS (mean opinion score from listeners), word error rate when the generated speech is transcribed |

Benchmarks tell you about a model in general. For a product, you'll always end up building your own evaluation set; the [Evals station](/learn/evaluation/) shows how.

<Interview>
<QA q="What's the difference between a discriminative and a generative model?">
A discriminative model learns the decision boundary between classes, p(y|x). A generative model learns the data distribution itself, p(x) or p(x|condition), so it can sample new data.
</QA>
<QA q="Why did diffusion models overtake GANs for image generation?">
Diffusion trains stably with a simple loss, covers the full variety of the data instead of collapsing to a few modes, and reaches higher quality. Its old weakness, slow sampling, has largely been fixed by latent diffusion, better samplers and distillation.
</QA>
<QA q="What does the guidance scale do in Stable Diffusion?">
It controls classifier-free guidance: how strongly sampling is pushed toward the prompt. Higher values follow the prompt more literally but reduce diversity and can over-saturate; lower values are more varied but may drift from the prompt.
</QA>
</Interview>

<Takeaways>
- Generative models learn the data distribution so they can create new samples.
- LLMs are autoregressive; VAEs give smooth latent spaces; GANs are sharp but unstable; diffusion leads in images and video.
- Latent diffusion plus text guidance is how modern image generators work.
- Evaluate with modality-specific metrics, then with your own task-specific evaluation set.
</Takeaways>
